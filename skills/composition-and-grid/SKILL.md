# Composition and grid

## Archetypes (pick one per poster; vary between events) <!-- core -->
- **Centred stack** (align center): formal ceremonies, very short content. Logo, title, details on one axis.
- **Header row + left column** (align left, HeaderRow): logo on one side, date/time/venue on the other, then a left-aligned title and body.
  Reads like an editorial page; good for workshops and talks.
- **Title low** (flow_valign bottom, align left): the upper half is art or a hero; the big title and the details sit in the lower third.
  Good when there is a strong image or generated background.
- **Split** (Columns): title block in one column, details or an icon list in the other, divided by a hairline. Good for dense content.
- **Big word** (BigWord): one short word or number as the graphic (a year, 30, JOIN), details in a band. Good for celebrations.
- **Tab and rows** (TabBanner, IconRow, InfoBar): a top strapline tab, a middle row of icons or columns, a bottom band. Good for
  recruitment and admission.

## Alignment and grouping <!-- core -->
One alignment axis per block: a left poster aligns every block to the same left edge; a centred poster to the centre. Do not mix within a
block. Proximity groups: lines that belong together are closer to each other than to anything else; the gap between groups is at least
twice the gap between lines inside a group.

## Where the eye goes
Western reading order is a Z on image-led posters (top-left identity, top-right date, diagonal to the title, bottom call to action) and a
vertical scan on text-led ones (title, date, details, action). Put the call to action where the scan ends: the bottom band.

## Thirds and balance
Place the focal point on a third line, not dead centre, unless the poster is deliberately symmetric. Balance visual weight (big title
against a hero or a QR block), not just geometry. A heavy dark band at the bottom is balanced by a large title in the upper half.

## Rhythm
Use few distinct vertical gaps (small inside a group, large between groups) and keep them consistent. Equal gaps between unequal blocks
read as accidental.
