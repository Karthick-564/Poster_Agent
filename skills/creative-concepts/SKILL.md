# Creative concepts

## Concept first, components second <!-- core -->
Before listing components, decide in one short paragraph (this is design thinking, never printed on the poster) and put it in `rationale`:
1. The idea: what the event feels like in one sentence, from the brief's own words and mood.
2. The visual metaphor or structure that carries it: a big number, a hero object, a light-and-shade split, a grid of tracks, a single band.
3. The composition archetype (centred stack, header row, title low, split, big word, tab and rows) and why it fits the content amount.
4. The ground and palette roles: dominant ground, structural colour, the one accent and where it goes.
5. The type style (classic, bold, elegant, editorial, condensed) and what is the dominant element.
Then build the components so every choice serves that paragraph.

## Variation rules <!-- core -->
- Do not reuse the structure that is simplest to write (centred logo, title, details). Choose the archetype assigned to this poster unless
  the content cannot support it.
- Three variants of one brief must differ in at least two of: archetype, type style, ground (light, dark, art), accent role.
- Vary scale, not only colour: a different dominant element is a different poster.
- Ask what a designer would add that a template would not: a big word, a strong crop, an asymmetric split, a band that carries the date.

## Guard rails
The concept never adds copy. Every printed word comes from the brief or the brand profile.
