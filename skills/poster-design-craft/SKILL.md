# Poster design craft

## House style facts <!-- core -->
Measured on the catalogue (205 posters): 172 are 9:16 story (84 %), 13 are 4:5, 9 square, 11 print. About 158 use white or light grounds.
About 48 carry a QR code and about 101 a footer or header band. So: default format is story; light grounds are normal; a QR block and a
footer band are standard institutional furniture, not decoration.

## Genre policy <!-- core -->
- Institutional genres (admission, recruitment, workshop, conference, talk, placement, commemoration, achievement): structure is legal and
  encouraged: top tab, pill label, solid footer band, QR row, icon row, columns.
- Premium cultural genres (festival, concert, exhibition): fewer boxes. Type sits directly on atmospheric art; one accent; wide margins.
- Never fill space with ornament. Fill it with scale (a bigger title or hero), structure (bands, columns, rules) or a real image.

## Hierarchy ladder <!-- core -->
Rank the brief's lines before choosing components: primary = event name; secondary = date, time, venue, call to action; tertiary =
organiser, eligibility, contact. One dominant focal point. Title at 15-22 % of the poster height when it is short; secondary about a third
to a quarter of the title size; tertiary 1.4-2 % of the height. Never give a sponsor or contact line the weight of the date.
Checkable: the largest text is at least 1.6x the second largest; the title and date can be read from a 300 px thumbnail (glance test).

## Information groups <!-- core -->
Group content into what / when / where / who / how-to-join. At most about six groups plus the footer. One idea per row; do not merge
unrelated facts into one sentence. Related lines stay together and in the brief's order (a batch name stays with its years).

## Depth and layering
Back to front: ground or art; soft scrim or gradient where text sits on art; title; hero cutout (may overlap the title's lower edge by a
few percent); structure (bands, rules); small type. A hero covers 30-60 % of the poster height. Text over art never sits on the busiest
part of the art.

## Negative space
Empty space is a design element only when it is deliberate: it surrounds the focal point. Large unintentional gaps between blocks mean the
blocks are too small, not that more ornament is needed. Checkable: no empty region above 14 % of the poster, no empty band above 3.5 %.

## Thumbnail test
At 300 px wide, the title must be unmistakable and the date findable. If not, scale and contrast failed; fix those before anything else.
